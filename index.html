FAQ & terms

Data & privacy
Where is my data stored?

On this device only, in your browser's local storage. There's no account and no server — nothing about your trips is ever sent anywhere.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Once the app is open, it works fully offline — logging stays, checking dates, and calculating your 90/180 status all happen entirely on your device.

Backup
What happens if I lose my phone or clear my browser data?

Your trip data is gone unless you exported a backup first. There's no server copy to restore from — that's the trade-off for keeping your data private, but it means backing up is entirely on you.

How do I back up or move my data to a new device?

Go to Settings → Backup & restore → "Back up my trips" to download a JSON file. On your new device (or after reinstalling), use "Restore from backup" to load it back in.

The rule itself
What does "rolling period" mean?

It means the 180-day window that counts toward your limit isn't fixed to the calendar — it moves with you. On any given day, the app looks back at the previous 180 days and adds up how many of them you spent in Schengen. As days roll out of that window, they stop counting, even if you never left. That's why your days-left number can go up over time on its own — you're not spending down a fixed allowance, you're always looking at a fresh 180-day slice ending today.

What counts as a day in the Schengen Area?

Any calendar day you're physically present in the Schengen Area counts as a full day — including your entry and exit days, even if you only cross the border for an hour.

How is the 90/180 rule actually calculated?

The app looks at a rolling 180-day window ending on any given date and counts how many of those days you spent in Schengen. If that count goes over 90, you're over the limit. Tap "How is this calculated?" on Home or the Safe Trip Checker to see the day-by-day math behind any number.

Do side trips outside Schengen (UK, etc.) count?

No. If part of a longer trip takes you outside the Schengen Area — a UK leg, for example — mark those days as excluded when logging the stay in Calendar, and they won't count toward your 90-day total.

App mechanics
How do I export my trip history for a visa application?

Go to Settings → Export trip history. "Export as CSV" gives you a spreadsheet-friendly file; "Print / save as PDF" gives you a formatted summary to hand to a border official or include with a visa application. Neither is the same as the JSON backup — use Backup & restore for that.

ETIAS
What is ETIAS, and does it affect my 90/180-day count?

ETIAS (European Travel Information and Authorisation System) is a separate travel authorization, not a visa. Starting late 2026, most visitors from visa-exempt countries — including the US, UK, and Canada — will need to apply for one online before traveling to the Schengen Area.

Here's the part that matters for this app: ETIAS doesn't change the 90/180 rule. It's a pre-travel authorization step layered on top of the existing rule, not a replacement for it. Once you're approved, your stay is still governed by the same 90-days-in-any-180-day-window limit this app tracks. Getting ETIAS approved doesn't extend your allowed days, and it doesn't reset your count.

ETIAS applications aren't handled through this app — apply directly at the official EU site. This page is here to explain how it fits with your day count, not to process your application.

Last checked: 12 Aug 2026. ETIAS's launch date has shifted before, so treat the timing above as current-best-information rather than fixed — check etias.europa.eu for the authoritative date.
